## Scope

A fresh adversarial whole-list pass was completed over all 99 noncommander slots of the exact SHA-bound approval candidate. The existing approval-candidate artifacts were treated as working evidence only, not as proof that the list was optimal. Every slot was challenged against current role requirements, the v8.1.4 Davros candidate pool, lower-ranked candidates with unusually strong text, current EDHREC optimized/Dalek evidence, and meaningful alternatives outside the obvious ranking leaders.

## Commander identity restated

Davros rewards causing opponents to lose at least 3 life during the Davros player's own turn, then converts that qualification at the beginning of the controller's end step into a 3/3 menace Dalek plus a draw-or-discard villainous choice for each qualifying opponent. The deck therefore prioritizes:

1. reliable own-turn 3-life qualification;
2. repeated/copy Davros end-step triggers;
3. discard conversion so the villainous choice is not a clean escape hatch;
4. Dalek/artifact/menace combat pressure;
5. enough interaction, protection, mana, and card flow to make those engines function at Bracket 4 rather than replacing them with a generic Grixis shell.

## Whole-list structural audit

The exact candidate contains 34 lands and 66 nonlands including the commander. Its nominal nonland average mana value is approximately 3.20; that figure overstates effective cost because Blasphemous Act and The Dalek Emperor have substantial cost reduction mechanics. The mana package contains 34 lands plus Sol Ring, Mox Opal, Arcane Signet, Fellwar Stone, all three Grixis Talismans, Ancient Tomb, Jeska's Will, Enthusiastic Mechanaut, and Mana Drain's conditional next-main-phase mana. This is sufficient for the present curve without adding slower three-mana rocks.

Artifact density remains high enough to support Mox Opal, Padeem, Tezzeret, Ashad, artifact-preserving sweepers, Cybermen Squadron, and the combat package. The current interaction suite is intentionally broad: premium countermagic, free interaction, targeted creature/enchantment/artifact removal, flexible bounce, and artifact-asymmetric sweepers. The list does not need another generic interaction slot at the expense of a commander engine.

Card advantage is distributed through Davros itself, Sygg, Tinybones, Geth's Grimoire, Waste Not, Cult of Skaro, Jeska's Will, Court of Ambition/monarch, Dauthi Voidwalker, Death in Heaven, Ensnared by the Mara, and Tezzeret recursion/value. Geth's Grimoire is specifically retained because it converts the discard branch of Davros's villainous choice into a draw trigger, meaning a qualifying opponent's normal Davros choice often produces a card either way.

Protection is not limited to generic boots: Padeem protects Davros because Davros is an artifact, Plaza of Heroes protects legendary creatures, and Deflecting Swat / Fierce Guardianship protect key board states while remaining premium interaction. Lightning Greaves and Swiftfoot Boots remain justified because the deck is materially worse when Davros or a principal artifact engine is repeatedly removed.

The direct or conditional qualification package includes Boltwave, Loyal Subordinate, Maestros Charm, Court of Ambition plus Entropic Battlecruiser, Perforating Artist, Sycorax Commander, Dalek Drone, Exterminate!, Cult of Skaro's Jast mode, Tezzeret, Delete at sufficient X, Missy, Wound Reflection, Torment of Hailfire, menace/artifact combat pressure, and other choice-dependent lines. The package is redundant without becoming a generic burn deck.

Davros multiplication is supplied by Roaming Throne, Strionic Resonator, Scientist Supreme of A.I.M., Auton Soldier, and The Valeyard's villainous-choice multiplication. These effects differ materially rather than functioning as interchangeable copies.

## Candidate-exhaustion red team

The highest-ranked omitted cards were re-evaluated rather than dismissed by ranking alone.

- **Cybermat** remains a good low-cost artifact attacker, but its threshold contribution is combat- and opponent-specific and it does not beat the current direct qualification, interaction, or engine slots.
- **Laser Screwdriver** is useful utility but is a three-mana rock in a list already carrying efficient two-mana fixing and faster mana; no current utility slot is weaker enough to justify slowing the mana package.
- **Unlicensed Disintegration** is the strongest omitted interaction challenger because it can destroy a creature and deal exactly 3 to that creature's controller while an artifact is controlled. It still loses the final slot comparison: the deck already has dense premium removal, the 3 damage matters for Davros only when used during our turn, and cutting Weeping Angel or another artifact creature would reduce reusable artifact/Alien/Ashad/Cybermen/Tezzeret synergies for a one-shot effect. It remains a high-quality alternate, not a material upgrade.
- **Golbez, Crystal Collector** offers cheap artifact-entry surveil and conditional end-step recursion/life loss. It is strong, but its 8-artifact drain condition also depends on a suitable creature card being returned and does not replace the cleaner threshold, protection, interaction, or draw engines already occupying the comparable low-cost slots.
- **Fateful Discovery** can draw heavily from artifact entries, but five mana is a real curve cost and the deck already converts both Davros branches into card advantage through Davros plus Geth's Grimoire while also carrying multiple cheaper draw engines. It is redundant rather than superior.
- **Nanogene Conversion** can create a large one-turn number of Davros copies from an established creature board, but it also turns our other creatures into Davros until end of turn, temporarily removing The Valeyard, Roaming Throne, Scientist Supreme, Padeem, and other creature-engine text during the exact end-step window where those abilities matter. Its ceiling is spectacular but its board-state dependence and internal cannibalization keep it below the current stable multiplier package.
- **Quantum Misalignment**, **Spark Double**, and **Irenicus's Vile Duplication** are efficient commander-copy options. They do not displace Auton Soldier: an Auton Soldier copying Davros is nonlegendary and an artifact, retains myriad, is protected by Padeem, contributes to artifact-count payoffs, can receive Ashad's casualty if it is the first qualifying artifact spell, and can use its myriad attack copies to deal 3 combat damage to additional opponents before those tokens are exiled at end of combat. The current package already has sufficient copy redundancy.
- **Kroxa, Titan of Death's Hunger**, **Pain Magnification**, **Painful Quandary**, **Let's Play a Game**, **Rankle's Prank**, **Blood Tithe**, **Flame Rift**, and **Slagstorm** all touch the life-loss/discard axis, but either depend on additional setup, occur at poorly aligned timing, impose symmetric costs, or are less efficient/flexible than the retained exact-threshold package.
- Generic high-power options such as Rhystic Study, The One Ring, additional universal tutors, and extra fast mana would raise generic card quality but would move the deck toward the same Grixis optimized shell used elsewhere. None is necessary to reach Davros's natural Bracket-4 ceiling, and none beats the commander-specific value of the retained slot it would have to replace.

## Important retained-card decisions

**Weeping Angel** survives despite being less obvious than Unlicensed Disintegration. It is a flash artifact creature and Alien, functions as reusable combat interaction, contributes to artifact density, can be copied through Ashad when applicable, benefits from Cybermen Squadron, and becomes a direct threshold combat piece when Cyberman Patrol grants it afflict 3. The one-shot removal alternative does not produce a net material improvement once these interactions are counted.

**Geth's Grimoire** survives because it directly attacks the choice structure of Davros: when an opponent chooses discard instead of allowing a Davros draw, Grimoire can replace that escape hatch with another draw trigger.

**Sygg, River Cutthroat** survives because its 3-life threshold mirrors Davros at only two mana and supplies additional end-step card advantage, including on opposing turns.

**Padeem, Consul of Innovation** survives because protecting artifacts includes protecting Davros itself, while the deck's artifact mana values frequently turn on the upkeep draw clause.

**Wound Reflection** survives its six-mana cost because it is both a finisher and a rules-relevant qualification tool: its beginning-of-end-step trigger can be ordered above Davros so earlier sub-threshold life loss is doubled before Davros resolves.

**The Master, Gallifrey's End** does not return. Its nontoken-artifact-creature death requirement is materially narrower than the deck's primary Dalek-token game plan and there is no dedicated sacrifice engine to force it reliably.
