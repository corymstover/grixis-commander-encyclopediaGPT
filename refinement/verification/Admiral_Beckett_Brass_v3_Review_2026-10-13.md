## Blueprint and commander necessity

Admiral Beckett Brass requires **three distinct Pirates to deal combat damage to the same player during the turn** to steal a nonland permanent at the beginning of the controller's end step. Commander-first optimization prioritizes repeatable connection, evasive Pirate density, attack support, token production, card advantage, and protection. Removing Beckett removes the deck's defining repeated permanent-theft conversion, although the Pirate combat shell remains functional.

## Proposed changes (not approved)

1. **OUT Coastal Piracy → IN The Indomitable.** Historical user-approved proposal is a starting point, not current approval. Both draw for each creature dealing combat damage. The Indomitable additionally offers a 6/6 trampling crewable Vehicle and a conditional graveyard casting permission with three tapped Pirates and/or Vehicles. It costs full {2}{U}{U} and follows normal timing; the draw is mandatory and artifact removal is a tradeoff. Current Oracle, blue identity and Commander legality were verified in the prior proposal. Role-for-role upgrade supported; historical approval does not substitute for current approval.
2. **OUT Glint-Horn Buccaneer → IN Zephyr Singer.** Zephyr Singer is a 3/4 flying/vigilant Pirate with convoke and grants permanent flying counters to creatures that convoked it. Three ground Pirates can convoke Zephyr (when untapped), receive flying counters, and connect more reliably on subsequent turns to satisfy Beckett's three-Pirate theft threshold. Glint-Horn is a Pirate with a repeatable rummage/damage engine, but the list lacks a focused discard engine; its activated ability works only while attacking and costs {1}{R} plus a discard. Zephyr is one mana more expensive before convoke and tapping creatures to cast it can sacrifice a current-turn attack. The tradeoff favors commander-specific repeatable evasion rather than incidental damage/discard.

## Red-team findings

1. **Zephyr Singer timing risk:** convoke requires untapped creatures and can prevent an attack in the casting turn. This is real; its counters pay off on later attacks, so do not treat it as immediate evasion on the same turn.
2. **Pirate-density and card-advantage risk:** cutting Glint-Horn preserves the Pirate count; replacing Coastal Piracy with a Vehicle does not. The list still retains Bident of Thassa, Kindred Discovery, multiple Pirate-based draw effects, and a substantial Pirate creature core.
3. **The Indomitable compulsory draw:** drawing many cards can become risky in long games, and artifact removal may be more common than enchantment removal. The graveyard cast is not free or instant speed.
4. **Generic Grixis goodstuff:** fast mana, premium interaction and tutors remain, but do not replace the three-Pirate connection plan; they support it.
5. **New-card freshness:** Jhoira, Weatherlight Corsair (2026-10-02) was explicitly considered rather than omitted from the older v8.1.4 candidate ranking.
