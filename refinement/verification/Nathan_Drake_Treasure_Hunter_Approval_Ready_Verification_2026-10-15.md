## 5. Nathan's cast-during-resolution window

Nathan's attack ability has no duration attached to the permission to cast a spell. The spell is therefore cast while the attack trigger itself is resolving, if the player chooses to use that permission.

Current Comprehensive Rules 608.2g confirms that when a resolving effect specifically allows a player to cast a spell during resolution, the player follows the casting process during that resolution; the new spell becomes the top object on the stack and the original ability continues resolving before players receive priority.

Consequences applied to this candidate:

1. Nathan still requires the spell's costs to be paid unless another effect says otherwise.
2. Nathan's static mana-fixing applies to spells the controller **does not own**; it does not turn the controller's own exiled top card into a five-color spell.
3. A land exiled by Nathan cannot be chosen as the spell because lands are not spells.
4. Mana generated by attack triggers such as firebending can be ordered to resolve before Nathan's trigger and then spent on the spell Nathan permits.
5. Each separate Nathan attack-trigger instance resolves independently and can cast one eligible spell from the cards exiled by that instance.

Result: **PASS**.
