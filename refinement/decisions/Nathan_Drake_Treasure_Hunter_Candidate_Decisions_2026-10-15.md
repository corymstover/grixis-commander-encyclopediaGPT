## Important retained-card decisions

- **Black Cat, Cunning Thief — KEEP.** The historical Outrageous Robbery swap remains rejected as a strict upgrade. Black Cat selects two cards from the top nine, leaves a Rogue body, and now benefits from Roaming Throne. Robbery is flexible and scalable but does not clearly dominate this role.
- **Interdimensional Web Watch — KEEP.** Its mana can pay for spells cast from exile, directly addressing Nathan's unusual need to pay for a stolen spell during an attack-trigger resolution.
- **Nalfeshnee — KEEP.** It copies a spell cast from exile and therefore directly doubles a successful Nathan cast.
- **Passionate Archaeologist — KEEP.** Two mana is an efficient rate for a direct exile-cast damage payoff; Keeper of Secrets is the redundant expensive version that leaves.
- **Stolen Strategy / Plargg and Nassari / Tasha — KEEP.** These remain independent theft engines so the deck functions through commander removal while still feeding the same stolen-card payoff network.
- **Brotherhood Regalia / Lightning Greaves / Swiftfoot Boots — KEEP.** Repeated attacking matters more than combat damage itself; these pieces keep Nathan alive and/or grant haste with much lower opportunity cost than Rogue's Passage/Aqueous Form redundancy.
- **Premium free interaction — KEEP.** Nathan is a commander-centric combat engine; losing a small amount of theft density to maintain Fierce Guardianship, Deflecting Swat, Deadly Rollick, Force of Negation, and cheap counters is strategically justified at Bracket 4.

## Candidate exhaustion — notable rejected alternatives

- **Outrageous Robbery — REJECT as Black Cat replacement.** Flexible instant-speed top-X theft, but Black Cat's selection from nine plus Rogue body makes the historical proposal an efficiency trade rather than a clear upgrade.
- **Chain Reaction — REJECT.** Candidate rank 1, but Blasphemous Act is generally the stronger sweeper in this shell; ranking score alone does not justify redundancy.
- **Hurl Through Hell — REJECT.** Strong theft/removal, but the interaction suite is already dense and Hostage Taker/Chaos Warp cover broader strategic needs.
- **Party Thrasher — REJECT.** Convoke applies only to noncreature spells cast from exile and attacking creatures are often already tapped during Nathan's window; Web Watch provides more reliable mana for any spell from exile.
- **Sakashima of a Thousand Faces / Spark Double — REJECT.** Both can create another Nathan, but require a four-mana copy body and another creature to attack. Roaming Throne supplies an immediate passive additional trigger without haste requirements.
- **Strionic Resonator / Lithoform Engine — REJECT.** Valid trigger-copy effects, but repeated activation mana directly competes with the mana Nathan needs to pay for the stolen spell during resolution.
- **Seize the Day / Combat Celebrant — REJECT.** Valid extra-combat redundancy, but Karlach is repeatable each turn, does not need to attack herself, and untaps Nathan as part of the trigger.
- **Aggravated Assault — REJECT.** Repeatable in theory but five mana per additional combat is a heavy ongoing tax and pushes toward a generic extra-combat combo package rather than Nathan's natural attack engine.
- **Spider-Verse — REJECT.** A slower once-per-turn outside-hand copy engine; Fire Lord Azula is cheaper and can copy every spell cast while she is attacking.
- **Access Tunnel — REJECT.** Nathan's power can be modified above three and the activation remains expensive; Shizo is one mana to activate and the deck already retains Regalia/boots.
- **Additional generic exile/theft engines — REJECT unless they beat an actual retained slot.** The baseline already has substantial theft density; the strategic bottleneck is multiplying Nathan and paying for his immediate spell, not finding more five- and six-mana theft cards.

## Final red-team result

The final serious pass specifically challenged the four new nonland engine cards, the eight-land mana-base package, retained generic theft engines, interaction density, and the decision not to add more trigger-copy/extra-combat redundancy.

No alternative produced a material improvement without a larger opportunity cost in mana, speed, commander dependence, or strategic identity.
